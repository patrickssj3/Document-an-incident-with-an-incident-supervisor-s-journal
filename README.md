# Incident Handler's Journal – Ransomware Attack on a Health Care Clinic

## Scenario

A small U.S. primary-care health clinic had a security incident on a Tuesday morning at about 9:00 a.m. Employees couldn't open files such as medical records, and a ransom note appeared on their screens. The note said an organized group of unethical hackers had encrypted the company's files and would sell back the decryption key for a large sum.

The attackers got in through **targeted phishing emails** carrying a malicious attachment. Once an employee downloaded it, malware was installed, which let the attackers deploy **ransomware** across the network. The clinic had to shut down its computer systems and call outside organizations for help.

## What I did

- Analyzed the incident details and recorded a dated, numbered journal entry.
- Documented the incident with the **5 W's** framework:

| Question | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Who** | An organized group of unethical hackers known for targeting the healthcare and transportation industries |
| **What** | A ransomware attack encrypted critical files, including patient medical records |
| **When** | Tuesday, approximately 9:00 a.m. |
| **Where** | A small U.S. primary-care health clinic (employee workstations and company network) |
| **Why** | Targeted phishing emails with a malicious attachment gave the attackers access to deploy ransomware |

- Recorded additional notes on prevention and response:
  - **Prevention:** phishing awareness training, email attachment filtering, and regular offline backups
  - **Ransom decision:** paying doesn't guarantee recovery and can encourage more attacks
  - **Compliance:** because patient data was affected, the incident should be reviewed for **HIPAA** breach-notification obligations

## Skills demonstrated

- Incident documentation and reporting
- Incident analysis using the 5 W's
- Understanding of phishing and ransomware attack vectors
- Awareness of healthcare regulatory requirements (HIPAA)
- Recommending preventive security controls
